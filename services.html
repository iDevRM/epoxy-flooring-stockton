Garage Floor Epoxy

The bread and butter. A flake or solid-color system laid over a ground slab, sealed with a clear topcoat that handles hot tires. Bare concrete in a garage does two things you notice every day: it throws dust onto everything you store out there, and it soaks up every oil drip until degreaser stops touching the stain.

Left alone, a stained porous slab only gets harder to fix. Oil that has migrated deep enough into the concrete becomes a bond-breaker, which means more grinding later and a bigger number on the quote. Homeowners converting a garage into a gym or workshop need this one first — the floor has to change before anything else does.

Metallic Epoxy Floors

Pigment suspended in clear resin and worked by hand while it is wet, so the finished floor has depth and movement instead of a flat color. No two are the same. This is the finish people pick for home gyms, showrooms, and front-facing retail spaces where the floor is part of the room rather than just the thing under it.

It is the most labor-intensive system we install and the most sensitive to a rushed slab — every dip and trowel mark shows in the reflection. Anyone comparing contractors should ask how much grinding and flattening is planned, because a rushed metallic almost always means a skipped grinding step.

See how metallic epoxy flooring works. Call us today for metallic epoxy flooring.

Commercial & Warehouse Epoxy

High-build systems for shops, warehouses, distribution space and light industrial floors around the Port of Stockton, Arch Road and the Airport Way corridor. Thicker coats, chemical resistance, and where it is needed, anti-slip aggregate broadcast into the topcoat.

A failing warehouse floor is not a cosmetic issue. Dusting concrete gets into product and equipment, spalled joints wreck pallet jacks and forklift wheels, and a slick spot near a wash bay is a liability sitting on your floor. We schedule commercial work in phases or on weekends so the operation keeps running.

Concrete Grinding & Floor Prep

Sometimes prep is the whole job: stripping a failed coating, opening a sealed slab, flattening trowel ridges, or grinding down a patch so a new floor sits flush. We also do this as a standalone service for contractors who want the slab profiled properly before their own crew coats it.

Skipping it is the single most common reason coatings fail in this area. Acid etching cannot remove the silicone and tire polymer that soaks into a garage slab, and a machine-troweled floor leaves a weak layer of cement paste on top that no coating can grab. Ignore either one and you are paying twice.
